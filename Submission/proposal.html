— Summary

The AIKN basketball court is a well-used outdoor space on campus, but it has nowhere to sit. Players' friends, teammates and visitors stand on bare earth at the edge of the slab. AIKN Courtside adds permanent, safe and colourful seating that turns the court into a small arena for practice, club games and campus events.

The design places four stepped concrete stands, two on each sideline, each with 40 moulded seats in 4 rows. The sections are named Imanzi, Jamaica, Imena and Intare, and each has its own seat colour, so every stand has an identity students can rally behind. Aisle stairs with handrails divide the stands, guardrails protect every raised edge, and new LED floodlights let the court be used safely after dark. The end walls carry carved portraits of Fred Swaniker and H.E. Paul Kagame with their words, and an illuminated ALU / AIKN COURT sign on the outside. The stands are built from durable, locally available materials, reuse the existing slab, and need very little maintenance.

01 The site today

Field visits found a sound but tired court:

  • No spectator seating. People stand or sit on the ground, which wears the grass to bare soil and turns muddy in the rainy season.
  • Faded markings and a weathered surface. The court lines are hard to read.
  • Limited lighting. Two street-type poles do not evenly light the playing area for evening use.
  • Strengths to keep: a level slab in good condition, mature shade trees, an existing paved path to the court, and an open view over the Kigali hills.

02 Design concept

Courtside seating should feel like part of the court, not something added beside it. The stands sit 1.8 m outside each sideline so spectators are close to the game, and the stepped rows lift every seat above the one in front for a clear view. A gap of about 3.7 m between the two stands on each side holds the aisle stairs and the team chairs, and keeps sight lines and circulation open at half court.

05 Construction approach

The stands are simple, robust masonry and concrete work that a local contractor can build in about 8–10 weeks, alongside ALU Facilities. Building one side at a time keeps part of the area usable for as long as possible.

  1. Survey and approvals (week 1). Confirm levels and soil conditions, set out the stands from the sidelines, and have a structural engineer and ALU Facilities review the design.
  2. Groundworks (weeks 2–3). Excavate and cast the strip footings, and lay the block perimeter and riser walls for the first side.
  3. Tiers (weeks 3–5). Backfill and compact in layers, then cast the reinforced treads and aisle stairs bay by bay, with drainage falls and a rear channel drain.
  4. Second side and end walls (weeks 4–6). Repeat for the other side; build and render the end walls.
  5. Court renewal (weeks 6–7). Repair cracks and apply the acrylic coating and new lines, while the concrete cures. Seats and rails are fitted only after the treads reach working strength.
  6. Fit-out and handover (weeks 8–10). Install seats, handrails, guardrails, section names and floodlights; plant the borders; carry out a final safety inspection.

The timeline is indicative and depends on contractor availability, weather (avoiding the heaviest rainy weeks for concrete work) and approvals. Students can help with the mural, section names and planting, so they share ownership of the result.

06 Safety

  • Comfortable, regular steps. 0.40 m risers with 0.85 m treads for seating rows; separate aisle stairs with a 0.20 m rise and 0.34 m going, so nobody has to climb the seating rows.
  • Handrails and guardrails. Handrails on both sides of every aisle stair. A 1.1 m guardrail with mesh infill runs along the back of each stand (the top tier is 2.0 m above ground) and down both open sides.
  • Slip resistance and visibility. Broom-finished treads, contrasting yellow nosings on every edge, and floodlighting for safe movement after dark.
  • Clear zones. The stands are 1.8 m clear of the sidelines and the end walls are 2.2 m behind the baselines, giving players run-off space. For competition use, FIBA asks for at least 2 m of clear space; if the site allows, the stands can move out by 0.2 m.
  • Exits. Every seat is within a few metres of an aisle stair or an open end, with level routes to the existing paved path.
  • Accessibility. Four level wheelchair bays with companion space at the ends of the stands, reached from the paved path without steps.
  • Rounded details. Chamfered concrete edges, capped tube ends and recessed fixings reduce injury risk.

07 Durability and maintenance

  • Reinforced concrete with 30 mm cover and good curing; drainage falls stop water from standing on the treads.
  • Hot-dip galvanised and powder-coated steel resists rust in Kigali's two rainy seasons.
  • UV-stabilised, colour-fast seats are designed for years outdoors. They are bolted individually, so a damaged seat is a five-minute swap, not a rebuild.
  • An anti-graffiti sealer on the walls and front faces makes cleaning easy.
  • Simple maintenance plan: a monthly check of rails, anchors and seats; cleaning drains before each rainy season; repainting nosings and lines yearly; recoating the court about every 5 years.

08 Sustainability

  • Reuse, don't rebuild. The existing court slab, hoop positions, paved path and mature trees are kept. No demolition waste.
  • Less cement. The tiers are soil-filled inside block walls, with thin concrete treads, instead of solid concrete. Soil excavated for the footings is reused as fill, and cement replacements (such as fly ash or slag blends) can be used where suppliers have them.
  • Local materials and labour. Blocks, aggregate, steelwork and seats can come from Kigali suppliers and fabricators, which cuts transport and supports local businesses.
  • Low-energy lighting. LED floodlights on timers, with an option for solar-powered fittings.
  • Water and planting. Rainwater from the treads and court drains to planted beds of native shrubs around the stands. These replace today's bare, eroded soil.
  • Recyclable at end of life. Polypropylene seats and steel rails can be recycled; recycled-content seats are preferred.

10 How it will feel

The design was modelled at full scale in Unity 6 (1 unit = 1 m) to test sight lines, step sizes and lighting. The model includes a 90-second voiced story film (an ALU host and two investors touring the court, two student players, two coaches, a narrator, day turning to night as the floodlights and sign switch on) and an interactive mode where visitors can play on the court and switch between day and night. It shows how the court could become a social heart of campus.

11 Assumptions and next steps

  • Dimensions come from the field visit and the 3D model, and should be confirmed with a measured survey.
  • Footings, reinforcement and guardrail fixings should be designed and signed off by a structural engineer.
  • A cost estimate can be prepared with ALU Facilities once the layout is approved. The design can also be phased: one side first (80 seats), then the second side.
